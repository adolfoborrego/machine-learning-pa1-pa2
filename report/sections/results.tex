\section{Results}

\subsection{PA1: Non-Probabilistic Classification}
\subsubsection{All Features}

With all features, the Neural Network has the highest F1 (0.4738) and balanced
accuracy (0.6765). SVM has the highest accuracy and precision but much lower
recall (0.2241). The remaining models have lower F1; their complete metrics
appear in Table~\ref{tab:pa1-all}.

\subsubsection{Univariate Filter}

Decision Tree leads this strategy in F1 (0.3809) and balanced accuracy (0.6347).
It and K-NN improve relative to their all-feature results; SVM, Neural Network
and RIPPER lose F1 after univariate selection.

\subsubsection{Multivariate Filter}

The Neural Network leads this strategy in F1 (0.3902), followed by Decision
Tree (0.3748). K-NN, SVM and RIPPER fall below their all-feature F1 scores;
SVM recall drops to 0.0511.

\subsubsection{Wrapper}

The RIPPER wrapper leads all reduced configurations in F1 (0.3996) and balanced
accuracy (0.6557), selecting a mean of 2.6 original predictors per outer fold.
Under the tree-based wrapper, Neural Network has the highest F1 (0.3919), while
K-NN improves from 0.2115 to 0.3146 relative to its all-feature F1. None of
these configurations exceeds the full-feature Neural Network.

\subsubsection{Comparison Across Strategies}

Table~\ref{tab:pa1-all} reports the ten-fold means for all 20 configurations;
Figure~\ref{fig:f1} shows the F1 pattern. The full-feature Neural Network
remains ahead of the best reduced configuration, the RIPPER wrapper.
K-NN, Decision Tree, SVM and Neural Network retain 15 of 51 transformed columns
under selection, a 70.59\% reduction. The corresponding RIPPER wrapper count
is measured in original predictors.

\subsection{PA2: Probabilistic Classification}
\subsubsection{All Features}
Full-feature Logistic Regression has the highest F1 among the standard PA2
classifiers (0.5373) and the highest AUC (0.8375). Mixed Naive Bayes has the
highest recall of all PA2 configurations (0.6453), alongside lower precision
and a larger Brier score. Table~\ref{tab:pa2-all} gives the exact metrics for
all four classifiers under each strategy.

\subsubsection{Univariate Filter}
Mixed Naive Bayes leads this strategy in F1 (0.4600) and maintains relatively
high recall (0.6033). QDA improves over its all-feature F1, whereas Logistic
Regression declines. Thirteen original predictors recur in all ten folds,
including overtime, job level and company tenure.

\subsubsection{Multivariate Filter}
CFS selects 3--11 original variables per fold (mean 7.8). Job level, overtime
and stock-option level appear in all ten subsets.
Mixed Naive Bayes leads this strategy in F1 (0.3962), below its all-feature
result, while its Brier improves.

\subsubsection{Wrapper}
Logistic Regression has the highest reduced-feature PA2 F1 (0.4696). Wrapper
searches retain roughly 13--15 original predictors on average, depending on
the classifier, but none exceeds the strongest full-feature baseline.

\subsubsection{Metaclassifiers and Overall Comparison}
Stacking obtains the highest PA2 F1 (0.5394) and lowest Brier (0.1846), though
its F1 advantage over full-feature Logistic Regression is marginal. AdaBoost
has a competitive AUC but a much larger Brier; Bagging has lower F1 and recall.

Table~\ref{tab:pa2-all} reports all 16 standard-classifier configurations, while
Table~\ref{tab:pa2-meta} gives the three metaclassifiers. Across both tables,
full-feature Logistic Regression has the highest AUC and Mixed Naive Bayes
the highest recall.
Figures~\ref{fig:pa2-f1} and~\ref{fig:pa2-brier} contrast F1 with
probability-vector error.
These rankings use observed cross-validation means and do not establish
statistically significant differences.
