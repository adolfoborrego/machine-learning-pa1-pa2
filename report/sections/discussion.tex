\section{Discussion}
\subsection{PA1 Performance and Dimensionality}
Given the class imbalance in Table~\ref{tab:dataset}, accuracy must be read alongside minority-class metrics. The full-feature SVM combines accuracy 0.8653 and precision 0.8363 with recall 0.2241 (Table~\ref{tab:pa1-all}): its positive predictions are relatively precise, but it identifies only a small proportion of employees leaving. The full-feature Neural Network has the highest observed PA1 F1 and balanced accuracy, although recall 0.4219 still indicates substantial missed attrition cases.

Selection does not universally improve performance. K-NN benefits from univariate and wrapper selection, while multivariate selection lowers its F1. Removing dimensions changes its neighbourhoods, which may help when useful features are retained; the experiment does not establish this mechanism. Decision Tree improves moderately with the filters. Every reduced Neural Network configuration loses performance relative to its complete representation.

The filters assess feature--target association, with the multivariate filter also penalising redundancy: the univariate subset retains both overtime indicators, whereas the multivariate subset retains only \texttt{OverTime\_Yes}. For K-NN, Decision Tree, SVM and Neural Network, wrapper additions are scored with a Decision Tree's F1, so the selected representation is not independently optimised for each final classifier. These criteria can favour different subsets. The 70.59\% reduction is substantial, but 15 transformed columns need not represent 15 distinct employee characteristics.


RIPPER's rule-based predictions can be inspected through explicit IF--THEN
conditions. Its classifier-specific wrapper raises F1 from 0.2866 to 0.3996
and provides the highest reduced-feature F1, while the complete Neural
Network remains best overall. The wrapper averages 2.6 original predictors,
whereas the filters select 15 transformed columns. Different units and the
bounded two-fold/three-step search prevent attributing these differences
solely to classifier choice. The overlap tables describe descriptive full-data
selector fits, whereas RIPPER's wrapper selections vary across outer folds.

The induced rules describe positive predictions through explicit conjunctions,
rather than feature retention alone. For example, the first outer training-fold
wrapper model includes the rule \texttt{OverTime=Yes} AND
\texttt{StockOptionLevel=0} AND \texttt{JobRole=SalesExecutive}; a matching
employee is classified as attrition. Another rule in that same model requires
\texttt{StockOptionLevel=0} and \texttt{JobRole=SalesRepresentative}, without
an overtime condition. Thus overtime is not required by every positive rule.
Across wrapper folds, overtime is selected in all ten, job role in eight and
job level in three; stock-option level appears in two, while company tenure,
department and work-life balance each appear in one. These are selections,
not condition frequencies or causal effects. Conditions within each rule are
joined by AND; rules are joined by OR, with no matching rule predicting no
attrition. Fold-specific rule sets illustrate model behaviour and should not
be presented as a single universal full-data rule set.

\subsection{Integrated Performance and Probability Quality}
Full-feature Logistic Regression in PA2 exceeds PA1's full-feature Neural Network in observed F1, with similar recall (Tables~\ref{tab:pa1-all} and~\ref{tab:pa2-all}). Its additive log-odds form and default regularisation offer a comparatively simple probabilistic model. Retaining all predictors may preserve jointly useful information, but this experiment does not establish a mechanism. PA1--PA2 comparisons combine classifier and representation differences, so they do not isolate an effect of probabilistic modelling.

Aggressive filtering can discard complementary information. PA2's univariate ranking scores predictors individually, while CFS favours relevance with low redundancy rather than classifier F1. CFS yields compact subsets but may omit jointly useful information. QDA's univariate improvement is consistent with reduced covariance-estimation demands, although it remains below Logistic Regression. The bounded classifier-specific wrapper likewise fails to recover the strongest complete-feature F1. PA1 and PA2 dimensionality reductions use different feature units.

Mixed Naive Bayes detects more minority-class cases at the cost of lower precision and higher probability-vector error. Its conditional-independence assumption may be restrictive for related career and compensation predictors, although this experiment does not establish the cause of its probability error. Higher recall does not imply better probability quality. AdaBoost's competitive AUC but much larger Brier likewise separates ranking discrimination from probability accuracy.

Stacking adds an internal fitting stage yet improves F1 and Brier only marginally over Logistic Regression, with unchanged recall and slightly lower AUC. Its related linear base learners may offer limited complementary information. Without a formal paired comparison, the differences do not establish a meaningful advantage. Full-feature Logistic Regression remains competitive on discrimination, probability quality and simplicity; Mixed Naive Bayes serves a recall-focused objective, while CFS offers compactness. No configuration dominates all objectives.

\subsection{Interpretation of Employee Characteristics}
Four predictors are selected in at least eight folds by both PA2 filters: \texttt{OverTime}, \texttt{JobLevel}, \texttt{StockOptionLevel} and \texttt{YearsAtCompany}. Overtime describes working conditions; job level describes organisational position; stock-option level represents a compensation attribute; and company tenure describes experience within the organisation. Their recurrence indicates that these complementary aspects repeatedly support predictive discrimination under different selection criteria. It does not establish that any attribute causes attrition, nor does selection alone reveal the direction or magnitude of an association. Overtime and stock-option level also appear in all three PA1 descriptive selections.

PA2's stable univariate selections further retain age, job role, involvement, monthly income and career-experience variables, supporting a multidimensional predictive interpretation. Related measures of professional level, compensation and experience should not be interpreted independently. PA1 agreement counts come from separate full-data fits, whereas PA2 frequencies count outer training-fold selections; neither is a causal importance ranking.

The existing descriptive analysis provides context for overtime: attrition is 30.53\% among employees with overtime and 10.44\% among those without it. These rates support interpreting overtime as associated with attrition-related classification patterns, rather than as a cause of leaving. \texttt{Age}, \texttt{JobSatisfaction}, \texttt{NumCompaniesWorked} and \texttt{TotalWorkingYears}, each selected by two strategies, add demographic, satisfaction and career-experience information.

Dummy categories must be interpreted together at original-variable level. For example, \texttt{OverTime\_Yes} and \texttt{OverTime\_No} encode the same employee characteristic; different retained \texttt{JobRole} categories still refer to job role. Agreement across methods makes the selected representation easier to discuss, but does not by itself provide a ranked importance measure or explain individual Neural Network predictions.

\subsection{Limitations}
The IBM dataset is synthetic/fictitious, so these findings should not be treated as evidence about a specific real workforce. Class imbalance remains present and minority-class recall is limited. Hyperparameters and decision thresholds were not optimised; conclusions concern the chosen configurations. PA1 fixes reduced subsets at 15 transformed features; PA2 ranks or searches original predictors, retaining at most 15. Neither setup establishes a globally optimal subset.

Numerical nominal codes in standalone LDA/QDA introduce representation dependence without meaningful ordering. QDA's instability inside stacking illustrates the sensitivity of covariance estimation to the available representation and sample size. PA1's wrapper and PA2's stacking fit preprocessing before their internal folds, as disclosed in Methodology; outer held-out folds remain excluded from all fitting. Cross-validation provides honest estimates for fixed configurations, but choosing the highest observed mean among many configurations may favour sampling variation. No independent external validation or statistical significance claim is provided. Logistic Regression coefficients provide supplementary descriptive insights into feature associations, but they should not be interpreted as causal effects or explanations of individual predictions.
