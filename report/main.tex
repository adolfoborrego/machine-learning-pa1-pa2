\begin{abstract}

Employee attrition is studied as a binary supervised classification problem
using 1,470 observations from the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition \&
Performance dataset.

Practical Applications 1 and 2 compare K-NN, Decision Tree, Support Vector
Machine and Neural Network classifiers with Mixed Naive Bayes, Logistic
Regression, Linear and Quadratic Discriminant Analysis. Each family is
evaluated with all valid predictors, univariate and multivariate filters,
and a forward-selection wrapper; Bagging, AdaBoost and Stacking extend the
probabilistic comparison.

Predictive performance is estimated through stratified 10-fold
cross-validation, with preprocessing and selection fitted within each outer
training fold; the wrapper uses internal five-fold selection.

The full-feature Neural Network leads PA1 in F1 (0.4738). In PA2, full-feature
Logistic Regression obtains F1 0.5373 and the highest AUC (0.8375), while
Stacking marginally increases F1 to 0.5394 and achieves the lowest
probability-vector Brier score (0.1846). Full-feature Mixed Naive Bayes
provides the highest PA2 recall (0.6453). Feature selection offers smaller
representations but does not outperform the strongest complete-feature models.

Recurring predictors, including overtime, job level, stock-option level and
company tenure, support predictive interpretation without implying causality.

\end{abstract}
